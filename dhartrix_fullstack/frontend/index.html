Organic Farming Coach

Beginner-friendly steps for soil health, composting, integrated pest management and safer crop care.

Recommendations will appear here. Verify crop-specific rates with local agricultural extension guidance.

Compost / organic manure preparation basics

  1. Choose a shaded, well-drained site away from wells and watercourses.
  2. Layer plant residues with suitable animal manure or other locally approved nitrogen-rich material.
  3. Keep the pile moist like a wrung-out sponge, not waterlogged; protect it from heavy rain.
  4. Turn periodically to provide oxygen and monitor temperature and odour.
  5. Use only well-decomposed, mature compost; avoid fresh manure on edible crops close to harvest.
  6. Apply according to soil-test results and crop guidance; more is not always better.
Do not mix unknown chemicals or pesticide residues into compost. Organic inputs can also carry pathogens or cause nutrient imbalance if handled incorrectly.

Sensor recommendations and limitations

  • Use calibrated capacitive probes for soil-moisture trends; readings need soil-specific calibration.
  • For moisture distribution across a large plot, use multiple mapped sensor points and depths, not one probe.
  • Soil moisture does not directly tell the depth of groundwater. Borewell siting needs hydrogeological assessment and local permissions.
  • Water-level sensors for an existing borewell measure water level in that well; they do not prove where to drill a new borewell.
